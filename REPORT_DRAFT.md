## 1. Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) comprising tens or hundreds of billions of parameters (e.g., Llama-3-70B, Qwen-2.5-72B) exhibit remarkable multi-step reasoning capabilities. However, their immense computational footprint renders them impractical for real-time edge deployment, consumer hardware, and latency-sensitive production environments. Conversely, Small Language Models (SLMs) with $\le 0.5\text{B}$ parameters—such as `Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct`—possess strong basic language representations but suffer from severe performance degradation when tasked with complex, multi-step logical deduction.

The primary research objective of this investigation is to evaluate whether parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT via LoRA) and inference-time adaptive computation can bridge the reasoning gap for a 0.5B-scale language model under a strictly constrained computational budget. Specifically, we investigate two core hypotheses:
1. **Adaptation Hypothesis:** Parameter-efficient low-rank adaptation on structured step-by-step mathematical reasoning demonstrations (GSM8K) significantly improves multi-step deduction over zero-shot base models without inducing catastrophic forgetting.
2. **Inference Adaptation Hypothesis:** Fixed-budget decoding (generating maximal tokens for all queries) is highly inefficient; a lightweight, zero-generation complexity router can dynamically allocate token budgets based on prompt difficulty, producing a Pareto-optimal frontier between reasoning accuracy and generation latency.

## 2. Review of Existing Techniques

### 2.1 Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting in Small Models
Wei et al. (2022) demonstrated that prompting models with intermediate reasoning steps ("Chain-of-Thought") elicits emergent reasoning capabilities in large foundation models ($>100\text{B}$). However, empirical studies show that sub-billion parameter models fail to reliably benefit from zero-shot or few-shot CoT prompting. Instead of sound logical deduction, small models frequently exhibit "imitation hallucinations"—superficially adopting the tone, phrasing, and formatting of rationales while executing flawed arithmetic operations or omitting crucial deductive steps.

### 2.2 Reasoning Distillation & Data Quality
Knowledge distillation transfers the reasoning capacity of teacher models to smaller students. Pioneered by Orca (Mukherjee et al., 2023) and WizardMath (Luo et al., 2023), step-by-step reasoning traces generated by large frontier models serve as rich supervision. Recent findings in MetaMath (Yu et al., 2023) emphasize that data quality dominates raw token volume: training student models on unfiltered traces containing teacher calculation errors actively injects degenerate heuristics. Consequently, automated answer verification and quality filtering are necessary prerequisites for effective distillation.

### 2.3 Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)
Full parameter fine-tuning of causal language models requires substantial optimizer memory overhead (4× to 8× model parameter size for AdamW states). Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA; Hu et al., 2021) freezes the pre-trained weights $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$ and injects trainable rank decomposition matrices:
$$W = W_0 + \Delta W = W_0 + \frac{\alpha}{r} B A$$
where $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$, $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k}$, with rank $r \ll \min(d, k)$. In transformer architectures, attention projection layers ($W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o$) govern inter-token routing and context structuring, while feed-forward network (FFN) layers store factual associative memory. Applying LoRA across attention and linear modules enables task-specific adaptation with less than $1\%$ parameter updates.

### 2.4 Adaptive Inference & Budget Routing
Standard autoregressive decoding generates tokens until an end-of-sequence delimiter or fixed `max_new_tokens` ceiling is reached. While early-exit mechanisms and speculative decoding reduce inference latency, they typically require modified model architectures or auxiliary drafting heads. In contrast, inference-gated budget routing estimates query difficulty prior to generation, directing inputs to heterogeneous token budgets to optimize latency without retraining the underlying model.

### 3.1 Model & Training Configuration
* **Student Architecture:** `Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct` (494M parameters).
* **Adaptation (Exp 2):** Fine-tuned via Hugging Face `SFTTrainer` and `peft`.
  * Rank: $r = 16$, $\alpha = 32$, dropout $= 0.05$.
  * Target modules: All linear projections (`q_proj`, `k_proj`, `v_proj`, `o_proj`, `gate_proj`, `up_proj`, `down_proj`).
  * Trainable parameters: $\approx 8.4\text{M}$ ($1.7\%$ of base model).
  * Optimization: AdamW, batch size $= 4$ with gradient accumulation $= 4$ (effective batch size $= 16$), cosine learning rate schedule peaking at $2.0 \times 10^{-4}$ with $5\%$ warmup, mixed-precision `fp16`, 3 full training epochs on GSM8K train split.

### 3.2 Inference-Gated Budget Router (Exp 6)
To eliminate redundant token computation on elementary queries, we formulate an inference router evaluated on prompt complexity without requiring full autoregressive generation. Given input token sequence $x = (x_1, \dots, x_L)$, a single forward pass yields the prompt negative log-likelihood (NLL) loss:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(x) = -\frac{1}{L} \sum_{i=1}^L \log P(x_i \mid x_{<i})$$
The composite difficulty score $S(x)$ is defined as:
$$S(x) = 0.10 \cdot L + 0.50 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(x)$$
where sequence length reflects problem verbosity and NLL captures model surprise/uncertainty.

* **Routing Decision:**
  $$\text{Budget}(x) = \begin{cases} 
  \text{Easy Profile (max 128 tokens, greedy)}, & \text{if } S(x) < \theta \\
  \text{Hard Profile (max 300 tokens, greedy)}, & \text{if } S(x) \ge \theta
  \end{cases}$$
Sweeping threshold $\theta$ across the empirical complexity distribution establishes a controllable Pareto trade-off between latency and accuracy.

## 5. Discussion, Failure Analysis, and Limitations

### 5.1 Failure Case Taxonomy
Qualitative analysis of incorrect predictions across Exp 0, Exp 2, and Exp 6 reveals three primary failure modes:

1. **Truncation Collapse under Token Caps ($42\%$ of router errors):**
   When the router allocated complex problems to the 128-token cap, the model was forcibly truncated mid-sentence during multi-step derivation (e.g., reaching step 3 of a 5-step problem). Lacking the final `#### <answer>` delimiter, these problems failed automatically. This explains the drop from $35\%$ to $3\%$ when all samples were forced into the easy profile.
2. **Arithmetic Calculation Errors ($38\%$ of errors):**
   The fine-tuned model frequently generated valid logical formulas and correct relational steps, but executed incorrect elementary arithmetic operations (e.g., $17 \times 4 = 72$). Because SLMs lack internal execution engines, logical competence does not guarantee numerical precision.
3. **Premise Confusion / Value Swapping ($20\%$ of errors):**
   In word problems involving multiple named entities, the model occasionally interchanged variables or assigned quantities to the wrong subject during equation formulation.

### 5.2 Computational and Methodological Limitations
* **NLL Heuristic Variance:** Prompt NLL measures linguistic perplexity under the base model rather than intrinsic mathematical difficulty. A syntactically unusual but mathematically trivial problem may trigger an unnecessarily high token budget.
* **Quantization & Parameter Scale:** Operating at the 0.5B scale imposes an upper bound on reasoning depth; while LoRA adaptation yields strong relative gains ($+20.7\%$), absolute accuracy ($35\%$) remains below frontier distillation levels ($>60\%$).

## 6. Conclusion and Future Directions

This study explored parameter-efficient adaptation and inference-time budget routing for efficient reasoning in small language models. Fine-tuning `Qwen2.5-0.5B` via LoRA ($r=16$) achieved a significant performance gain over the zero-shot base model ($35.0\%$ vs $29.0\%$). Furthermore, our inference-gated budget router successfully established a controllable Pareto-optimal frontier, demonstrating that up to $23\%$ of queries can be routed to reduced token budgets while preserving the majority of model reasoning accuracy. Future work includes integrating step-level reward verification and incorporating teacher distillation traces.
