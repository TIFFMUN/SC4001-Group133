## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Problem Formulation and Motivation
Modern frontier Large Language Models (LLMs) comprising tens to hundreds of billions of parameters (e.g., Llama-3-70B, Qwen-2.5-72B) exhibit remarkable multi-step reasoning capabilities. However, their immense computational and memory footprints render them impractical for edge deployment, local client hardware, and latency-bounded production APIs. Conversely, Small Language Models (SLMs) with $\le 0.5\text{B}$ parameters—such as `Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct`—possess strong basic language representations but suffer from severe performance degradation when tasked with complex, multi-step logical deduction.

The primary research objective of this investigation is to evaluate whether parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT via LoRA), knowledge distillation, and inference-time adaptive computation can bridge the reasoning gap for a 0.5B-scale language model under a strictly constrained computational budget. Specifically, we investigate two core hypotheses:
1. **Adaptation & Distillation Hypothesis:** While direct adaptation on terse human demonstrations may induce computational length collapse, distilling step-by-step reasoning traces from large teacher models allows small language models to preserve their reasoning scratchpad and significantly outperform zero-shot baselines without catastrophic forgetting.
2. **Inference Adaptation Hypothesis:** Fixed-budget decoding (generating maximal tokens for all queries) is highly inefficient; a lightweight, zero-generation complexity router can dynamically allocate token budgets based on prompt difficulty, producing a Pareto-optimal frontier between reasoning accuracy and generation latency.

## 2. Review of Existing Techniques

### 2.1 Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting in Small Models
Wei et al. (2022) demonstrated that prompting models with intermediate reasoning steps ("Chain-of-Thought") elicits emergent reasoning capabilities in large foundation models ($>100\text{B}$). However, empirical studies show that sub-billion parameter models fail to reliably benefit from zero-shot or few-shot CoT prompting. Instead of sound logical deduction, small models frequently exhibit "imitation hallucinations"—superficially adopting the tone, phrasing, and formatting of rationales while executing flawed arithmetic operations or omitting crucial deductive steps. Furthermore, in-context exemplars consume precious context length and dilute the attention heads of small models.

### 2.2 Reasoning Distillation & Quality Filtering
Knowledge distillation transfers the reasoning capacity of teacher models to smaller students (Hinton et al., 2015). Pioneered by Orca (Mukherjee et al., 2023) and WizardMath (Luo et al., 2023), step-by-step reasoning traces generated by large frontier models serve as rich supervision. Recent findings in MetaMath (Yu et al., 2023) emphasize that data quality dominates raw token volume: training student models on unfiltered traces containing teacher calculation errors actively injects degenerate heuristics. Consequently, automated answer verification and quality filtering are necessary prerequisites for effective distillation.

### 2.3 Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA)
Full parameter fine-tuning of causal language models requires substantial optimizer memory overhead (4× to 8× model parameter size for AdamW states). Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA; Hu et al., 2021) freezes the pre-trained weights $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$ and injects trainable rank decomposition matrices:
$$W = W_0 + \Delta W = W_0 + \frac{\alpha}{r} B A$$
where $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$, $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k}$, with rank $r \ll \min(d, k)$. In transformer architectures, attention projection layers ($W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o$) govern inter-token routing and context structuring, while feed-forward network (FFN) layers store factual associative memory. Applying LoRA across attention and linear modules enables task-specific adaptation with less than $2\%$ parameter updates.

### 2.4 Adaptive Inference & Budget Routing
Standard autoregressive decoding generates tokens until an end-of-sequence delimiter or fixed `max_new_tokens` ceiling is reached. While early-exit mechanisms and speculative decoding reduce inference latency, they typically require modified model architectures or auxiliary drafting heads. In contrast, inference-gated budget routing estimates query difficulty prior to generation, directing inputs to heterogeneous token budgets to optimize latency without retraining the underlying model.

### 3.1 Model Architecture & Training Configurations
* **Base Student Model:** `Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct` (24 transformer layers, hidden dimension 896, 14 attention heads, 494M total parameters).
* **Supervised LoRA Adaptation (Exp 2):** Fine-tuned on the official GSM8K train split (7,473 human solutions) using Hugging Face `SFTTrainer` and `peft`.
  * Rank: $r = 16$, $\alpha = 32$, dropout $= 0.05$.
  * Target modules: All linear projections (`q_proj`, `k_proj`, `v_proj`, `o_proj`, `gate_proj`, `up_proj`, `down_proj`).
  * Trainable parameters: $8.44\text{M}$ ($1.71\%$ of base model).
  * Optimization: AdamW, effective batch size $= 16$ (per-device 4, gradient accumulation 4), cosine learning rate peaking at $2.0 \times 10^{-4}$ with $5\%$ warmup, 3 full epochs.
* **Distilled Student Models (Exp 3 & Exp 4):**
  * Teacher Model: Frontier model `Qwen-2.5-32B` hosted on Groq, generating full Chain-of-Thought rationales.
  * Exp 3 (D1): Student trained on all 522 teacher rationales.
  * Exp 4 (D2): Student trained on 408 answer-verified/filtered rationales.
* **LoRA Adaptation on Distilled Student:** LoRA adaptation ($r=16$) applied on the distilled student model checkpoint to investigate stacking parametric adaptation with distillation.

### 3.2 Two-Stage Evaluation Methodology & Token Budget Diagnostic

A central contribution of our experimental design is the structured two-phase evaluation framework:

#### Stage 1: 100-Sample Pilot Screening & Diagnostic Test Run
To rapidly evaluate prompt variants, baseline settings, and adaptation schemes under cloud GPU compute constraints, all initial configurations were screened on $N = 100$ samples with the standard generation limit of 256 tokens.
* Initial observations showed Exp 0 at 29.0%, Exp 1 at 23.0%, and Exp 2 at 35.0%.
* **The Token Truncation Discovery:** A diagnostic inspection of generation outputs and token counters revealed an unexpected bottleneck: **$68.0\%$ of baseline (Exp 0) generations hit the 256-token ceiling**.
* An unconstrained test run inspecting actual token length requirements revealed that `Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct` requires **300 to 500 tokens** to express multi-step mathematical derivations. When truncated at 256 tokens, generation was cut off prematurely before outputting `#### <number>`, falsely penalizing correct reasoning chains as incorrect.
* Furthermore, inspecting Exp 2 showed **severe length collapse** (averaging 112.9 tokens due to mimicking terse human GSM8K labels). Exp 2 appeared superficially superior at 256 tokens solely because its concise answers avoided truncation!
* Based on this finding, we updated the generation configuration from 256 to **600 tokens**, reducing truncation from $68.0\%$ to just $1.4\%$.

#### Stage 2: Full 500-Sample Scaling of Promising Configurations
With the 600-token budget established, compute was committed to scaling the primary and promising methods to **500 samples** on GSM8K (covering $37.9\%$ of the entire test set) and **1,172 samples** on AI2 ARC-Challenge (the full official benchmark).

### 3.3 Inference-Gated Budget Router Formulation (Exp 6)
To eliminate redundant computation on simple questions while preserving token headroom for hard problems, we formulated an inference router evaluated prior to generation. Given prompt $x = (x_1, \dots, x_L)$, a single forward pass yields the prompt negative log-likelihood (NLL):
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(x) = -\frac{1}{L} \sum_{i=1}^L \log P(x_i \mid x_{<i})$$
Composite complexity score:
$$S(x) = 0.10 \cdot L + 0.50 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(x)$$
Prompts with $S(x) < \theta$ receive the Fast Profile (max 128 tokens), while $S(x) \ge \theta$ receive the Deep Budget Profile (max 300/600 tokens).

### 4.4 Cross-Task Transfer Evaluation on ARC-Challenge (Exp 5)

To evaluate whether reasoning adaptation on mathematical problems induces catastrophic forgetting of general science logic, the distilled student model was evaluated zero-shot across the **entire official AI2 ARC-Challenge test set (1,172 samples)** using candidate sequence log-likelihood scoring.

* **ARC-Challenge Accuracy:** **$33.62\%$** (394 / 1,172 correct).

* **Comparison to Random Baseline:** Outperforms uniform 4-way random guessing ($25.00\%$) by **$+8.62\%$ absolute** ($+34.5\%$ relative).
* **Takeaway:** Mathematical CoT distillation preserves out-of-domain conceptual reasoning while delivering over $90\times$ faster inference than autoregressive generative decoding.

## 5. Discussion, Failure Analysis, and Limitations

### 5.1 Mechanistic Failure Case Taxonomy
Qualitative inspection of incorrect predictions across 500 samples revealed three primary failure modes:

1. **Length Collapse under Human Annotation SFT ($44\%$ of Exp 2 errors):**
   Fine-tuning on concise human labels trained the model to emit `<|im_end|>` after $\sim 113$ tokens. Rushing into immediate arithmetic caused hallucinations:
   * *Example:* $2 \times 2 = 4$ bolts of blue fiber (when problem stated 2 bolts), resulting in $4 + 2 = 6$ instead of $2 + 1 = 3$.
2. **Arithmetic Calculation Slips ($36\%$ of errors):**
   The model structured valid problem logic (e.g., $180 - 54$), but executed an incorrect elementary arithmetic slip ($180 - 54 = 99$). Sub-billion models lack internal execution engines, separating logical structure from arithmetic precision.
3. **Premise Confusion / Variable Swapping ($20\%$ of errors):**
   In word problems with multiple entities, the model occasionally interchanged variables or assigned quantities to the wrong subject during equation formulation.

### 5.2 Computational and Methodological Limitations
* **Pretraining Alignment Bias:** `Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct` has extensive prior alignment for discursive, verbose reasoning. Human SFT partially untunes this persona, whereas distillation reinforces it.
* **Quantization & Parameter Ceiling:** A 0.5B model has strict capacity limits; achieving $>50\%$ on GSM8K without external verifiers or tool use remains challenging.

## 6. Conclusion

This study explored parameter-efficient adaptation, reasoning distillation, and inference budget routing for small language models. 
1. **Pilot screening on 100 samples** exposed a major token truncation bottleneck ($68\%$ truncated at 256 tokens), motivating an upgrade to a 600-token budget.
2. **Scaling to 500 samples** revealed that human SFT induces computational length collapse ($31.2\%$, 113 tokens), whereas **knowledge distillation from an LLM teacher restores the reasoning scratchpad ($40.8\%$, 408 tokens)**, providing a $+9.6\%$ accuracy advantage.
3. The distilled student transferred zero-shot to **ARC-Challenge with $33.62\%$ accuracy** across 1,172 test samples at $130\text{ ms}$ latency.
4. The inference router established an empirical Pareto frontier, demonstrating up to $23\%$ computational savings with minimal accuracy loss.
